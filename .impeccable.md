## Design Context

### Users

SimplyStroke is for recreational golfers, primarily middle-aged men playing social rounds with friends. They care about keeping pace, enjoying the group, and remembering the count without turning golf into a data session. The core job is to record each shot with one easy tap on iPhone or Apple Watch, then get attention back onto the next shot. The homepage’s primary conversion is an App Store click that leads to a first recorded stroke.

### Brand Personality

Relaxed, direct, and quietly playful. The voice should feel like a dependable friend in the foursome: confident without sounding technical, lightly cheeky without becoming “golf bro” caricature, and always clearer than it is clever. The SimplyStroke logo and existing green/lime palette are locked. Product imagery must be authentic to the current app.

### Aesthetic Direction

An editorial golf field guide with real product behavior woven into the course atmosphere. Deep course green, crisp off-white, and rare lime accents should carry the page; neutral surfaces are green-tinted rather than beige. Photography, oversized display type, scorecard-inspired rules, and small content-specific line illustrations create rhythm without generic card grids. The signature motion is the app counting a stroke: a guided pointer presses the real ball target and the iPhone/Watch state responds. Supporting motion should explain state changes, not decorate every section.

Primary homepage and navigation CTAs use the approved B / High Contrast treatment: off-white buttons with dark-forest `#153c2c` text on dark sections, dark-forest buttons with off-white text on light sections, and an integrated green arrow panel with a lime arrow. Do not use the orange/amber experiment. Hero and closing download CTAs pair the Apple glyph with “Free download / on the App Store.” Keep the green/lime identity elsewhere; the compact mobile navigation CTA may omit the arrow panel to preserve room for the logo and menu.

### Design Principles

1. Show the actual app for key screens and interactions. Product behavior must match current iPhone and Apple Watch UI; compact scorecard-style diagrams may replace a full phone frame when the frame adds empty space without adding understanding.
2. Make the benefit felt. Lead with mental relief, one-tap counting, wrist scoring, and shared responsibility—not implementation details users already expect.
3. Keep one dominant action. App Store download buttons are primary; guest play and browser access are reassurance or quiet secondary paths.
4. Use visual rhythm, not repeated templates. Alternate immersive photography, dark editorial fields, and open light sections; avoid uniform icon-card grids and generic beige blocks.
5. Motion teaches. Autoplay may demonstrate the count and group flow, but manual interaction remains available and `prefers-reduced-motion` receives a stable equivalent.
6. Design for the tee box. Mobile text stays comfortably readable, controls meet touch-target guidance, and animations stay lightweight on a phone outdoors.
